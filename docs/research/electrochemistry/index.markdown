  <h1>Electrochemistry</h1>

  <img src="{{ '/assets/img/research-electrochemistry2.jpg' | relative_url }}" alt="Electrochemistry">

  <p>
    My master's research focused on Pt-based electrocatalysts for D-glucose oxidation
    in glucose fuel cells. Glucose is a renewable and biologically abundant fuel, but
    its electrochemical oxidation is kinetically slow because it involves multiple
    adsorption and oxidation steps on the catalyst surface.
  </p>

  <h2>Research Motivation</h2>

  <p>
    Many glucose oxidation catalysts work efficiently only in strongly alkaline media,
    which are not ideal for practical or biomedical applications. My research therefore
    aimed to understand and improve Pt-based glucose oxidation catalysts in neutral
    aqueous electrolytes.
  </p>

  <h2>Mechanistic Study</h2>

  <p>
    I used cyclic voltammetry and electrochemical quartz crystal microbalance (EQCM)
    measurements to investigate the Pt-catalyzed glucose oxidation process. The results
    suggested that glucose oxidation involves adsorption of glucose-derived
    intermediates, formation of Pt-OH species, and subsequent oxidation toward
    gluconolactone or gluconic acid.
  </p>

  <h2>Catalyst Design</h2>

  <p>
    Based on this mechanistic picture, I studied Pt/C-PtO<sub>2</sub> mixtures and PtPd alloy
    catalysts. These systems were designed to promote key surface oxidation processes
    and improve the catalytic activity of Pt-based electrodes in neutral solution.
  </p>

  <h2>Outcome</h2>

  <p>
    This study proposed two possible pathways for glucose oxidation on Pt-based
    catalysts: direct oxidation and OHads-mediated oxidation. PtPd catalysts were
    further evaluated in glucose-air fuel cells and showed improved fuel-cell
    performance.
  </p>
